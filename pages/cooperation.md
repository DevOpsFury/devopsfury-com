The **Technology Organization Effectiveness Assessment** helps leaders understand what is getting in the way of reliable operations and predictable software delivery — and decide which changes are worth considering first.

We look at how the organization works: ownership, collaboration, workflow, decisions, and the conditions teams operate in. This is not about assigning blame or recommending tools for their own sake.

# When might an assessment help?

It may be useful when incidents regularly displace planned work, delivery is hard to predict, or ownership of decisions and systems is unclear.

Other reasons to look closer may include knowledge silos, growing technical debt, teams under sustained pressure, or difficulty connecting technical investment to business goals. We do not assume all these issues exist in your organization. The assessment helps identify which, if any, are relevant.

# Who is it for?

For CTOs, VPs of Engineering, engineering leaders and managers, DevOps, SRE and Platform Engineering leaders, and technology business owners responsible for team effectiveness.

It is intended for leaders willing to examine how the organization works and consider possible improvements with their teams.

## When it may not be the right fit

This is not a technical audit or an employee evaluation. If you need only a review of code, infrastructure, or a specific tool — or want someone to identify who is at fault — this is not the right engagement. Implementation of all recommendations is not automatically included.

# What do we look at?

The assessment covers all five areas. We consider them in the context of your organization and the questions you want to answer.

1. **Reliability and operational risk** — how the organization identifies risks, responds to incidents, and learns from disruption.
2. **Software delivery and workflow** — what contributes to queues, delays, rework, handoffs, or unpredictable delivery.
3. **Organization, ownership, and collaboration** — whether ownership of decisions, systems, and outcomes is clear, and how cross-team dependencies affect work.
4. **Leadership, decisions, and priorities** — how priorities are set and decisions about risk, technical debt, and investment are made.
5. **People, skills, and workload** — how workload, skills development, and reliance on individuals affect the way teams work.

# What you receive

- **A review across all five areas** — a summary of observations and context, including limitations in the available information.
- **A distinction between observations and hypotheses** — so incomplete evidence is not presented as certainty.
- **Priorities and recommendations** — possible changes ordered by their relevance to the organization.
- **A PDF report** — delivered within 14 days of the final assessment meeting.
- **Suggested first steps for the next 30/60/90 days** — proposed sequencing, dependencies, and signals the organization may choose to monitor.
- **Three follow-up meetings** — approximately one hour each, held 30, 60, and 90 days after the assessment is completed.

The assessment provides a basis for decisions; it cannot guarantee a particular improvement in reliability, delivery speed, or financial performance. Decisions about implementing recommendations remain with your organization.

# FAQ

## Is the introductory call free?

Yes. The call is free and is intended to understand your context and assess whether the assessment is a fit. It is not a full consulting session, and it does not promise a ready-made solution during the call.

## How many people can participate?

The pilot is designed for a team of up to 10 people. We agree who should participate and how before the assessment begins.

## Do you need sensitive data or production access?

Access to production systems or sensitive data is not assumed. Before starting, we agree what information is needed and how it can be shared. An NDA can be signed before the engagement. Any specific information-sharing arrangements are agreed in advance.

## Is this a technical audit?

No. This is an assessment of how a technology organization works. It may consider technical context, but it is not automatically a code review, security test, or infrastructure audit.

## What happens after the report is delivered?

The report provides a summary and recommendations. We then meet at 30, 60, and 90 days to discuss progress. Your organization decides what to implement and remains responsible for those actions; implementation is not part of the assessment.

## Is this an evaluation of employees?

No. The purpose is to understand working conditions and organizational mechanisms — such as ownership, dependencies, priorities, and workload — not to judge individuals or assign blame.

Not without your prior consent. After the engagement, I will ask for feedback. I may also ask whether you would be comfortable providing a reference or allowing me to name your organization and use its logo. This is optional; nothing will be published without your permission.
